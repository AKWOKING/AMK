## 4 · GAP-FILLING

**What the video should have covered and did not:**
- **No disclosure that the three statistics come from TikTok itself.** They are presented as neutral
  findings ("stats that I came across recently which genuinely blew my mind") inside a video
  sponsored by the platform that produced them. That is the central audit finding here.
- **No geography.** Every figure is global/US-weighted. Nothing about whether any of it holds in
  francophone Africa.
- **No cost of the advice.** "Be on TikTok" is free but it is a daily time cost for a shop owner;
  she never prices it.
- **No measurement.** Nothing about how to tell whether the five tactics worked.

**Cameroon / WhatsApp-first / optician application:**
- **"Be on TikTok" is the most valuable free idea in this batch** — but for us it means *King* spends
  time on the app, which is a real labour cost against a 500 000 FCFA/month target. Worth doing
  before the two-week local-discovery test ruled in batch 1, not after.
- **Content that answers a question** maps directly onto optician queries: « faut-il une ordonnance
  pour des lunettes ? », « combien de temps dure un examen de vue ? », « quelle monture pour un
  visage rond ? ». DM Optique's FAQ already answers several of these on the site — the video
  content writes itself from the site's FAQ.
- **Influencer alignment over size** matters if we ever propose influencer work: a Douala micro-creator
  with 3,000 followers who wears glasses beats a 200,000-follower comedian.

**Counter-evidence:** the statistics are TikTok's own and describe **shopping** behaviour on a
platform with a shopping infrastructure. An optician's sale is an in-person eye exam and a fitted
frame — not an impulse checkout. Applying "67% inspired to shop" to a healthcare service would be a
category error, and we would not quote it to a client.
