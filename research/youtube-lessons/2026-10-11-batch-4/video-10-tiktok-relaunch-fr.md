## 4 · GAP-FILLING

**What the video should have covered and did not:**
- **That the account was purchased.** It appears once, mid-video, framed as "to be 100% transparent"
  — but the title's whole promise rests on the opposite.
- **That the footage is other people's.** Never named as a rights problem. He even speculates about
  which source account the original French account copies from, without noticing the implication.
- **That the income depends entirely on a reward programme with a country list.** He never mentions
  eligibility. A viewer in Cameroon would follow every step and earn nothing.
- **What happens when an account is flagged.** Buying accounts and re-uploading third-party footage
  are both removal risks; the video has no failure analysis.

**What he attributes growth to (the specific question):** not the content's quality, and not the
niche alone. He attributes it to **① the "additional rewards" unlocking from the fourth video
(RPM 0.10 → $2.84) and ② then doubling to two posts a day.** In his own numbers, virality came
early and was worthless: 200,000 views paid **$18**. The revenue arrived when the reward tier
changed, not when the views did. **For a Cameroonian account, lever ① does not exist** — so lever ②
would produce views with no revenue attached.

**The relaunch narrative structure (the genuinely transferable idea):**
> State a **number and a deadline** ("over €2,000 by the end of the month") → declare the **second
> goal** ("to show you it's not just luck") → promise **full transparency** → reveal the account,
> the niche, the strategy → walk **day by day**, including the bad early numbers (10-cent RPM,
> $18 for 200,000 views) → then the **final tally** against the original number.

That structure works because it is **a public experiment with a pre-registered result**, and
because it shows the losing days. **AMK has exactly this available:** the two-week local-discovery
test ruled on 11/10 (AMK's own TikTok first, zero client risk, hypothesis and result logged). It
could be run as a public 30-day experiment on AMK's own account — hypothesis stated up front,
numbers shown weekly, including the failures.

**Format:** **faceless and screen-led** — he films his phone and his analytics dashboard. No
production budget, no on-camera presence. That matches our constraints exactly (phone-first,
`research/PHOTO-LABO-PIXEL-8A-2026-09-24.md`).

**Cameroon / WhatsApp-first / optician application:**
- **The income premise is void here.** This video is the strongest confirmation yet of the batch-1
  ruling: **TikTok for Cameroon clients = discovery + WhatsApp handoff, never direct commerce.**
- **The structure is adoptable; the monetisation is not.** Strip the reward programme and the
  stolen footage, and what remains — a public, numbered, day-by-day experiment — is free, honest and
  exactly what our own account needs.
- **One practical tip worth keeping for me:** *"one ChatGPT conversation per account"* — keep a
  separate context per client so themes don't bleed. It matches our per-client file discipline.

**Counter-evidence:** batch 2's video 6 argues **quality over frequency** and warns algorithms
measure "how many people care." Video 10's whole engine is 1–2 posts/day of re-edited footage — the
opposite. Both can be true (they optimise for different things: he optimises reward-programme volume,
we optimise client acquisition), but for **our** goal his cadence is the wrong lever.
