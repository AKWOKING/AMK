## 4 · GAP-FILLING

**What the video should have covered and did not:**
- **The platform names.** The single most damaging omission: without them, no limit, term or licence
  can be checked, and nothing he says is reproducible.
- **Licensing for commercial use.** Never raised once, in a video whose explicit purpose is
  producing content with these tools.
- **What a watermark signifies.** It is treated purely as an obstacle. For AI output it is often a
  provenance marker; removing it is a disclosure decision, not a cleanup step.
- **Total cost of a real workflow.** 4 credits does not make a video with retakes. Batch 3's audited
  figure (a real clip needs 4–6 attempts plus edits) shows why.

**Cameroon / WhatsApp-first / optician application:**
- **For client deliverables: no.** King's ruled scope is **"no synthetic imagery of your business."**
  A generated image of an optician's shop, staff, frames or patients is exactly what that forbids,
  and for a business handling eyes it is a misrepresentation risk (`AMK-DESIGN-SKILLS.md` §25/§32,
  `research/PHOTO-LABO-PIXEL-8A-2026-09-24.md`).
- **For AMK-owned content: still no, for a different reason.** The batch-3 rule permits labelled
  generated visuals for AMK content where no real footage exists — but it cannot be satisfied here,
  because the **licensing is unknown** and the free allowance is 4 clips. There is no need this
  serves that real photography cannot.
- **The watermark-removal step contradicts our own adopted rule.** Batch 3 adopted "the visual is
  labelled as generated." Removing the marker is the opposite act.

**Counter-evidence:** the video's framing is that free tiers are sufficient. Two batches of audited
data say otherwise — Google Flow's documented 50 credits/day with a SynthID watermark (batch 3), and
here 4 credits and 5 generations. Free AI video is a **prototyping** budget in both datasets.
